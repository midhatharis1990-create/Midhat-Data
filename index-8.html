Two examples of turning financial records into something more useful than a spreadsheet: a reconciled financial position, and a Power BI analysis of revenue, margin, collections, and pricing.

01 Reconciliation

The profit number wasn't the whole story.

A small trading business had a monthly P&L that looked healthy on its own, but it had never been reconciled against the full ledger. Receivables and a supplier balance were tracked separately, leaving the real payable exposure unclear.

What I did

Reconciled sales, cost of sale, receivables, and supplier balances across the ledger, separating reported operating profit from the amounts actually owed to and by the business.

Key numbers

Gross profitPKR 6.8M
Net profit (P&L)PKR 2.9M
Customer receivablesPKR 380K
Supplier payable balancePKR 2.3M
Net payable position–PKR 1.92M

Result: the reported profit was real, but reconciliation surfaced a PKR 1.92M net payable exposure that the monthly P&L alone did not show.

02 Power BI

Turning 68 job records into a pricing and collections story.

A car detailing and paint-protection-film (PPF) business had raw job and payment records across 68 jobs. The challenge was to see where revenue and margin sat, which jobs were more likely to remain unpaid, and where pricing or service mix could improve.

What the analysis surfaced

Instead of raw dashboard screenshots, this case study highlights the business questions, metrics, and decisions the analysis surfaced.

Revenue concentration
PPF is the dominant category

PPF generated approximately PKR 5.7M, the largest single share of revenue.

Collection risk
Smaller jobs pay slower

Lower-value jobs were roughly 2× more likely to have pending payments — a case for targeted follow-up.

Pricing opportunity
PPF priced well under benchmark

Average SAC PPF ticket was ~$732 versus a ~$2,000 global benchmark.

Service-growth opportunity
Ceramic coating underrepresented

Shown at 3% adoption versus a 19% global benchmark — room to review.

Takeaway: PPF is the main revenue engine, while smaller high-volume services deserve more targeted collection follow-up. The benchmark analysis also flags PPF pricing and ceramic-coating growth as areas worth reviewing.
